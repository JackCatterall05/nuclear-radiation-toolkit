Results observations

1. Linear attenuation coefficient (mu):
Decreases with increasing energy.
Greater at each energy for lead than concrete.
Lead is the better absorber of radiation.
Lines for concrete and lead get closer as energy increases showing factor between mu decreases with energy (log-log graph, hence factor difference).

2. HVL:
Increases with energy.
Absorption is less effective as photon energy is greater, so greater thickness of material is required to reduce intensity by 50%.
Concrete worse absorber so requires larger thickness. 
Lines for concrete and lead get closer as energy increases showing factor between HVL decreases with energy (log-log graph, hence factor difference).

4. Required shielding thickness:
Same as for HVL, just greater thicknesses to reduce to 1%.
Lines for concrete and lead get closer as energy increases showing factor between thicknesses decreases with energy (log-log graph, hence factor difference).

5. Concrete/lead thickness ratio:
General trend is for ratio to decrease with energy. 
But in graph there is a minimum at 1.5 MeV.
The values for the ratio for energies 1.25 MeV, 1.5 MeV and 2 MeV are close (4.99, 4.87 and 4.98). 
The thickness values have a statistical nature, possibly explaining why the ratio appears lower at 1.5 MeV than 2 MeV.
At energies greater than 1.25 MeV it is possible the ratio values level out and decrease at a sufficiently slow rate that the random nature of the thickness values could cause the ratio to be slightly lower at a lower energy. 
We need to explore how the ratio develops at energies greater than 2 MeV.
We need to take more data between 1.25 MeV and 2 MeV, especially if the ratio does increase after 2 MeV, to more precisely find the energy where the ratio is a minimum. 
We should quantify the statistical errors so we know if the deviations are consistent with the errors.
